# Design: Compliance Audit Report — proving demand before building the accountability loop

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-02
Branch: main
Repo: 1996Lucifer/medical
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

The camera-based face/PPE detection pipeline (recently optimized for smoother, lower-latency
inference) is technically solid, but the underlying question — is this a company worth building
around — hasn't been tested against a real buyer yet. The ask that triggered this session was
"how do we make the camera/PPE detection powerful enough to be a real AI hospital management
system," but the diagnostic surfaced a sharper problem: there's no named person on the buyer side
who has committed to this, and the camera-vision layer alone is not differentiated in the
market it would be selling into.

## Demand Evidence

Strongest evidence available: a verbal agreement from a decision-maker at one facility — no
signature, no payment, no scheduled pilot date. When pushed to name the single person who owns
this internally, the answer was "everyone, especially the owners," and on a second push: "it's
genuinely a group decision right now." No named champion exists. This is real interest, not yet
demand.

## Status Quo

The target facility handles PPE/compliance today via (a) manual guard walk-throughs — a real,
ongoing staff-hour cost — and (b) existing CCTV that records but is never actively reviewed for
compliance. The camera infrastructure and the guard payroll already exist; neither is being used
to catch violations proactively. That gap is the real wedge, not the sophistication of the
detection model.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

No single named target user exists yet — this itself is the top risk (see Premises). The
narrowest wedge that doesn't require a champion to already exist: a one-time **Compliance Audit
Report** run against the facility's own existing footage, handed to whoever is closest to a
decision-maker, with the explicit ask "who owns acting on this, and can we get 15 minutes with
them this week." The report itself is the tool for finding the champion, not a product feature
for an already-identified buyer.

## Premises

1. Raw PPE/face detection accuracy is not a moat — it's commoditized by funded competitors
   (Protex.ai, Ambient.ai, Pelco, VCA Technology, Athena Security all claim 95%+ accuracy today).
2. The real differentiation opportunity is the closed loop — violation → accountable action →
   audit trail, tied to real staff identity/attendance/RBAC — which this codebase has partial
   infrastructure for already (compliance tokens with revocation in `compliance_engine.py`, live
   security WebSocket alerts in `routers/security.py`, RBAC, attendance tracking).
3. Camera/feed-integrity monitoring (detecting a blind/frozen/tampered camera — partially built
   already as tamper heuristics in `camera/worker.py`) is an underserved, concrete pain point in
   this market, not a nice-to-have.
4. No named internal champion at the pilot facility is a bigger near-term risk than any missing
   feature. This is the premise that should gate what gets built next.
5. **Revised during session** (originally: bundle the camera-compliance and AI-agent/RAG pitch
   together — rejected after cross-model challenge). The combined vision (camera events as the
   AI agent's first real dataset) is still the long-term product story, but pitching it now, to a
   leaderless buying committee, raises the bar for consensus rather than lowering it. Pitch
   narrow first — the compliance/audit-report wedge alone — and let the agent capability be the
   upsell once a real, named owner exists and asks for more.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Independent Claude subagent cold-read (no visibility into this conversation, given only a
structured summary):

- **Steelman:** a hospital operations layer that turns unreviewed camera infrastructure into an
  accountable compliance loop, then reuses that same live event stream as the grounding data for
  a hospital AI assistant — one system, not two products, if sequenced correctly.
- **Key insight:** "it's genuinely a group decision right now," after two rounds of being pushed
  to name a person, is the most revealing answer in the session. Group-decision-with-no-champion
  usually means no one inside the building owns the pain enough to fight for budget or install
  access. Building more detection accuracy right now optimizes a product nobody there has
  committed to.
- **Premise it flagged as wrong:** the original premise #5 (bundle the pitch because "selling
  together is easier than selling separately"). That's a founder aesthetic, not a sales fact, and
  specifically risky given there's no champion — a combined ask is *heavier* for a leaderless
  committee to approve than a narrow one a single department head could say yes to alone.
  Disproof condition: a named decision-maker unprompted asks for the agent/chat capability as a
  condition of moving forward. Confirmation condition: pitch gets vaguer answers on "who signs
  for this," not sharper ones.
- **48-hour prototype suggestion:** don't write more detection code — build a static Compliance
  Audit Report generator on the existing pipeline (violations by zone/shift, guard-hours-
  equivalent, timestamped evidence clips as PDF), walk it in person to whoever's closest to a
  decision-maker, and ask directly who owns acting on it. A real "yes" is a named person with a
  calendar slot within days; anything vaguer is the same non-answer already on hand.

This directly led to revising premise #5 above.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Compliance Audit Report (minimal viable) — CHOSEN
Effort: S (human ~2-3 days / CC ~2-4 hrs) — contingent on the batch-mode spike below landing on
day one; treat "48 hours" (the cross-model estimate) as the aggressive case and 2-3 days as
realistic. Reuses the existing `vision_service_zones.py` event output (`ppe_events`,
`face_events`, zone data) — needs a thin batch-video runner wrapped around `process_frame()`
(the live pipeline was built/optimized for a streaming RTSP source, not arbitrary recorded
files/codecs/fps, so this is not assumed to be a zero-change drop-in; spike this first before
committing to the estimate — pass/fail: the runner processes a 10-minute sample clip from the
target facility's actual camera model, end to end, without manual intervention). Renders a static PDF: violations by zone/shift, a guard-hours-
equivalent estimate (default formula: violation count × avg. manual-check time, explicitly
caveated as an estimate unless the facility supplies real labor-cost figures), timestamped
evidence clips (10s pre/post the detected violation timestamp, delivered as separate MP4 files
referenced by filename+timestamp in the PDF — not inline-playable video, which PDF readers don't
reliably support). No new live infrastructure, no token system changes, no agent-layer work.
Before the report is finalized: manually spot-check a sample of flagged violations against the
source footage (batch-mode accuracy on a different facility's cameras/lighting isn't validated
by the live-pipeline work already done, and a wrong violation shown to the champion undermines
the exact credibility this wedge depends on).
Pros: fastest real-world demand test; forces the champion question directly instead of assuming
one exists; near-zero new infra risk since it's built on code that already works.
Cons: not a "product" yet; doesn't touch the agent/RAG vision; feels like a step back from the
live dashboards already built; carries real privacy handling work (see Open Questions/
Dependencies) that a pure detection feature wouldn't.

### Approach B: Full accountability loop (ideal architecture) — rejected for now
Effort: L (human ~3-4 weeks). Violation → auto-assigned action item to a named accountable role →
tracked resolution → audit trail, plus camera feed-integrity monitoring as a first-class alerting
feature. This is the actual differentiated product implied by premises #2/#3 — but committing
3-4 weeks to it before testing demand assumes those premises are correct without a real customer
confirming them. Rejected for now, not permanently — this is what gets built once Approach A
produces a named champion.

### Approach C: Camera-integrity-only wedge (creative/lateral) — rejected for now
Effort: S-M (human ~1 week). Lead with "find out which of your cameras are actually working"
(tamper/blind/frozen detection) as a standalone, low-controversy hook any facilities/security
head can approve without a compliance conversation, PPE/face detection becomes phase 2.
Pros: zero controversy, easy yes, sidesteps PPE-vendor commoditization entirely.
Rejected for now: undersells the compliance work already built, and doesn't directly test
whether PPE compliance (the actual pitch made to the facility so far) has real demand — it tests
a different, smaller claim instead.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A. It is the only option that tests the actual blocker identified in this session (no
named champion) rather than assuming the moat premises are correct and building toward an
undefined buyer. B and C are both legitimate follow-ups once A produces a real signal.

## Open Questions

- Whose footage does the audit report actually run on — does the facility with the verbal
  agreement have footage available now, or does this need to run on separately-sourced test
  footage first as a portfolio piece?
- What does "a real yes" concretely unlock — pilot access, a paid pilot fee, or just a scheduled
  meeting? Define this before walking the report in.
- If the audit report finds nothing alarming (facility is already reasonably compliant), what's
  the fallback pitch? (Possibly the camera-integrity angle from Approach C.)
- **Privacy/consent for evidence clips:** this is a hospital — footage will capture patients as
  well as staff, and handing the facility a PDF with identifiable video evidence is itself a
  compliance-sensitive act. Two separate consent questions, both unresolved: (1) consent to
  *generate* the report at all (a hospital may refuse to hand over footage for this purpose,
  which is a harder blocker than "footage isn't available yet"), and (2) consent to *share*
  identifiable clips once generated. If (2) requires blurring non-violating bystanders/patients,
  that's a real face-detection-and-blur pipeline on batch video — nontrivial CV work, not covered
  by the S-effort estimate above. Resolve both before generating anything, and if blurring is
  required, treat it as a separate, unestimated risk item that could blow the effort budget.
- **Guard-hours-equivalent formula:** undefined pending real data. Basis is likely
  (violation count × avg. manual-check time), but the facility's actual labor-cost/staffing
  figures aren't confirmed as available. If real figures can't be obtained before the report is
  delivered, the number must be explicitly caveated in the report as an estimate — presenting it
  as authoritative risks undermining credibility at the exact moment ("who owns this") the wedge
  depends on.
- **Shift boundaries:** source for the zone/shift breakdown is undefined — fixed time blocks
  (e.g., three 8-hour shifts) unless the facility can supply an actual schedule.

## Success Criteria

Within 2 weeks: a named individual (title + facility) has taken a specific calendar-scheduled
action in response to the audit report — not "let us discuss internally." That name is the gate
for starting Approach B. If the window lapses without a name: do not retry the same pitch with
the same group — fall back to Approach C (camera-integrity-only wedge) as a lower-controversy
re-entry point, or walk away from this facility and re-run Approach A against a different one.

## Distribution Plan

No new deployment pipeline needed — the audit report is a one-off deliverable (PDF/document)
handed directly to a person, generated by a script built on the existing FastAPI backend's vision
pipeline. Existing Docker/deployment setup for the live system is unaffected and unnecessary for
this step.

## Dependencies

- Access to real or representative footage from the target facility (or a suitable stand-in) to
  run the batch report against.
- The existing `VisionServiceZones.process_frame()` pipeline and event schema (already verified
  working via this session's earlier optimization pass) as the event source — but a new thin
  batch-video runner around it (recorded-file ingestion, not live RTSP) is in scope for this
  build, not assumed to be free.
- Facility-supplied (or explicitly-caveated-as-estimated) labor-cost/staffing figures for the
  guard-hours-equivalent number.
- Facility shift-schedule data, or agreement to use fixed time-block shifts as a stand-in.
- A PDF-rendering approach/library (not yet chosen) and a decision on evidence-clip privacy
  handling (see Open Questions) before any report is generated.

## The Assignment

Before writing another line of detection/vision code: build the Compliance Audit Report generator
(Approach A), run it against real or representative footage, and hand it in person to whoever is
closest to a decision-maker at the facility with the verbal agreement. Ask them directly: "who in
this building owns acting on this, and can we get 15 minutes with them this week?" A real answer
is a named person with a calendar slot within days. Anything vaguer is the same non-answer already
on hand — and the signal to keep pushing on this specific question before investing further build
time.

## Engineering Review Decisions

Locked during `/plan-eng-review` (2026-09-03), superseding the loose sketch in Approach A:

**Structure:** 3 separate modules under `backend/scripts/compliance_audit/` — `batch_runner.py`,
`aggregator.py`, `clip_extractor.py`, `report_renderer.py` (kept as distinct files, not collapsed
into one script, per explicit choice during scope review).

**Security [P0, fixes existing code too]:** `backend/main.py:118` mounts `/uploads` with NO auth
dependency and wide-open CORS — anything already saved there (including existing incident
snapshots from `camera/worker.py`) is publicly readable with no login. This must be fixed
(`dependencies=auth_dep` added to the mount, or equivalent) as part of this work, before any
Compliance Audit Report artifacts are generated. New report output/evidence clips go to an
authenticated-only location, never `/uploads`.

**State isolation:** the batch runner constructs a **fresh `VisionServiceZones()` instance per
video file** processed (not one reused instance). This is both correct (no track-ID/identity-cache
bleed between files) and cheap (the actual models are cached in the `ModelManager` singleton, not
per-instance — no re-warmup cost).

**Error handling:** per-frame `try/except` around `process_frame()` plus explicit handling of
`cv2.VideoCapture.read()` returning `ret=False` — skip and log, continue the run, report the
skipped-frame count in the output. A multi-hour batch run must not lose all progress to one bad
frame; it also must not silently swallow errors without surfacing the count.

**Zone bucketing:** the aggregator reuses `VisionServiceZones._classify_zone()` (via the same
per-file instance) rather than reimplementing polygon-matching logic — avoids a second,
drift-prone zone classifier.

**Throughput:** the batch runner **samples frames** (e.g. one every 1-2 seconds) rather than
processing every frame of the footage. A compliance audit doesn't need frame-perfect timing —
sustained violations (a shift without a mask) are the target, not brief ones — and full-frame
processing of up to 2 weeks of footage would take far longer than the 2-week demand-test window
this whole plan exists to protect.

**Testing:** despite no existing test framework in this repo (the `test_*.py` files at
`backend/` root are manual smoke scripts against a live server, not pytest), this new subsystem
introduces real pytest coverage — matches the stated "well-tested code is non-negotiable"
preference, scoped only to the new modules (not a retrofit of the rest of the codebase). See the
companion test plan: `~/.gstack/projects/1996Lucifer-medical/dj-main-eng-review-test-plan-20260903-001332.md`.

**Consent gate:** the batch runner refuses to run without an explicit `--consent-confirmed` flag
(or equivalent), operationalizing the still-open legal question in Reviewer Concerns #1 as a hard
code-level gate rather than a documentation note that's easy to skip under deadline pressure.

**Testing scope (revised after outside-voice challenge):** full pytest coverage across all 4 new
modules was reconsidered — an outside reviewer correctly flagged that investing in durable test
coverage for an explicitly throwaway/spike-gated script contradicts this plan's own core argument
(prove demand before investing further). Scoped down: pytest coverage applies to the `/uploads`
auth fix (real regression risk on existing, already-shipped code) and the `--consent-confirmed`
gate (the one failure mode — silently running without consent — that must never happen). The
batch runner/aggregator/clip extractor/report renderer get manual verification during the spike,
not full unit coverage, until the pilot proves demand and the tool graduates beyond a one-off.

**Legal/BAA sequencing (outside-voice challenge, accepted as documented rather than resequenced):**
an outside reviewer flagged that the HIPAA/BAA question (Reviewer Concerns #1) is plausibly the
actual critical path — if legal review takes longer than finding a champion, the 2-week demand-test
clock could lapse before any code runs, and nothing here currently tracks that risk on the same
timeline as the champion search. Decision: keep this as an open question rather than resequencing
work — engineering spike and legal conversation proceed in parallel, no hard dependency ordering
imposed. Recorded here explicitly so this remains a visible, deliberate risk acceptance rather
than an oversight if the 2-week window lapses on the legal side.

## Reviewer Concerns

Unresolved after 3 rounds of adversarial review (quality score 8/10 on the final pass) — carry
these into execution rather than treating the doc above as fully settled:

1. **HIPAA/BAA:** footage may contain PHI (patients visible). A signed Business Associate
   Agreement or equivalent legal agreement may be required before the facility can share footage
   at all — this is a legal gate, not just the UX consent question already covered in Open
   Questions. Verify with facility legal in parallel with the batch-runner spike, before
   committing further engineering time.
2. **No fallback if the batch-runner spike fails** its pass/fail bar — if it fails, halt and
   re-scope the effort estimate rather than proceeding on the original S estimate.
3. **Zone calibration for the target facility's actual camera layout** is real, unbudgeted work
   not currently accounted for in Approach A's effort estimate or Dependencies.
4. **Secure handling of footage in transit/at rest** (distinct from the consent question already
   raised) is unaddressed.
5. **Approach A's report format** (PDF + separate MP4 clips) reads as more settled than it is —
   it's contingent on the still-open PDF-tooling and clip-privacy decisions in Dependencies.
6. **Timestamp-to-clip extraction approach** (fixed-FPS decode, frame-index-to-wall-clock
   mapping) isn't specified — needed before an engineer can start the batch-runner spike.
